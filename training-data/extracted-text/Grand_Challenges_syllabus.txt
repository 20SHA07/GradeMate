GENS 101 Grand Challenges
(2 Lecture, 4 Instructional Studio – 4 Credits)
Prerequisite: None
Course Catalog Description:
This course surveys science and engineering disciplines and highlights interdisciplinary, real-world
applications, especially addressing Grand Challenge problems facing humanity. The course introduces
aspects of problem-solving ranging from the formulation of questions and problems to the testing of trial
solutions within a standard framework. Students discuss ethical, social, economic, environmental, and
technical aspects of Grand Challenges, and they contribute to collaborative and project-based activities.

Course Topics:

a. Team roles, coordination of work, introduction to project management
b. Effective meeting strategies and guidelines for conflict resolution

b. From information to ideas

a. Information gathering to define a problem/question
b. Situational contexts and constraints (physical, social, economic, environmental, and
ethical (provide methodology to apply))
c. Sub-problems and use of representations
5. Developing useful models and solution designs (4 lectures)
a. Identifying and/or developing applicable models

Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Lectures are 2 x 50 minutes per week and Studio sessions are 2 x 100 minutes per week. Students
prepare for class sessions by viewing assigned videos, websites, and/ or documents. Students maintain
individual portfolios for reflection on the choice of a major, Grand Challenge issues, and project work.
Students train individually on specific skills via course Tutorials. Teams of 4 to 6 students work
collaboratively on Studio Activities and a Grand Challenge project. Studio Activities expose students to
the entire problem definition, solution development, and testing process. For th eir selected topic, each
team develop s a problem statement, solution proposal, final oral presentation, and written report
(includ ing self and peer evaluations components).
Instructional Studio and Computer Resources:
Course materials including Portfolio assignments, Tutorial and Instructional Studio Activities, and
instructions for the course project, are available via university online platforms. Students need access to
the following software: Google Earth Pro and Tableau for Students. The Instructional Studio includes
the following equipment: Robotics kit s, Adhesive & Epoxy kits, 3D Printer, Electronics k its, Biomedical
kits, Cool Tool machining tools, Chemistry kit s, Biology kit s, Data A cquisition kits, and hand tools.

Course Policy on late Submission of Assignments:
 All assignments are due on the date and time stated. It is the students’ responsibility to familiarize
themselves with assessment details and deadlines.
 Late project assignments and tutorials will receive a credit reduction of 5% of the grade per day until it
is submitted. A completely missed project assignment or tutorial will receive a zero (0).
 No late submissions of pre-class graded activities and reflections will be accepted. There are no make-
ups for these activities. However, the lowest pre-class activity grade and reflection grade will be dropped
from the final grade calculation.
 Students absent from in-class/live online graded activities worth 5% or lower (i.e. studio activities) will
receive a zero (0) for the activity. There are no make-ups for these activities. However, to acknowledge
that some students may have an emergency and miss a class, the lowest studio activity will be dropped
from the final grade calculation.
Additional Information:
 Students are expected to attend the entire class on time and participate in activities to be considered
present.
 Students must bring their laptops and headphones to each class. Students unable to complete activities due
to not having working hardware will receive a zero (0) for the activity. There are no make-ups for these
activities.
 At the instructor's discretion, students receiving a grade of less than 70% for an individual assessment
worth 5% or higher might have the opportunity to revise or retake the assessment one time within a
specific timeline. Note: If a student resubmits an assignment, the maximum grade the resubmitted
assignment can be awarded is 70%.
 Students who are found to cheat or plagiarize (copy in any form) text, images, or other materials may
receive a zero or a grade reduction with a formal report sent to administration. Depending on the
classification of the violation, the case may be forwarded to the Academic Integrity Council for review.
Therefore, students are advised to consult the Handbook for all policies related to plagiarism and other
forms of cheating, especially ACA 3500.
