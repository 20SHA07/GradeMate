GENS 300 Career Preparation
(1 Studio, 1 Credit)
Prerequisite: GENS 100, Sophomore Standing
Course Catalog Description:
This course equips learners with the mindset and skills for both internships and future careers. It includes
employability workshops, group discussions, and one-on-one guidance from a local employability and
labor market specialist. This activity-based course is focused on field exploration, CV and digital profile
development, and professional communication. Course delivery is a blend of in-person and asynchronous
online learning experiences.
Textbook: None
Reference Materials: What Color is Your Parachute for College by Katharine Brooks, Publisher: Ten
Speed Press, 2021, ISBN: 9781984857569.
Course Topics:
1. Introduction to the course (1 lecture)
2. Preparing skills for employability: Knowledge, experience, and personal attributes (1 lecture)
3. Emerging professional profile: Researching and writing a bespoke CV (1 lecture)
4. Interview skills development (2 lectures)
a. Communication- active listening
b. Resilience (responding to feedback)
5. Job (or internship) search techniques (1 lecture)
a. Setting SMART career goals
6. Experiential learning communities (2 lectures)
a. On-campus employment opportunities
b. Updating industry-based e-Portfolio
7. Creating an online professional profile (2 lectures)
a. Networking through LinkedIn and Glassdoor
b. Trends in the UAE job market
8. Teamwork: skills and strategies (2 lectures)
a. Effective communication and empathy
b. Team building and collaboration
c. Critical thinking and decision-making
9. Work ethic and business etiquette (2 lectures)
a. Professionalism and self-discipline
b. Reflection on changed paradigm, and enhance the proactive skill set
10. Intercultural attributes: Adaptable in changing environments (1 lecture)
Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Studio sessions are 1 x 100 minutes per week. Sessions involve interactive activities in a blended learning
environment that includes online lessons and classroom lectures facilitated by the course instructor.
Assessments will be formative via, online knowledge checks, digital portfolios, peer assessment,, and an
online summative assessment.

Classroom Policies
Discipline is one of the main components of your academic and professional success.
Please make every effort to arrive at the class periods in a timely fashion. Students arriving late to
class are a distraction to both the instructor and the other students in the classroom. As per the
University policies, any student arriving late to class is to be considered absent.
Please ensure that you sign your name on the Attendance Sheet which is handed-out at the beginning
of each class period. Please do not sign-in for absent friends. If you do not sign the Attendance Sheet,
then you will be marked “Absent” in the Banner.
If you are absent for any lecture, it is your responsibility to cover the missed material.
Homework Policy
Homework is your responsibility. Homework exercises have been selected to enable you to practice the
material covered in the class and to self-test your learning of the material. You are expected to have
completed the homework exercises, and to have sought assistance with any of the exercises which have
caused you difficulty, before the next class period. Assistance is readily available in Instructor Office
Hours.
