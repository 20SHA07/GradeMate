Instructor Policy on Exams, Assignments, and Quizzes:
Homework will be assigned approximately every 2-3 weeks. These are a fundamental part of the
learning process. While students are encouraged to discuss these problems collaboratively, the
submitted homework by each student must represent their own independent work and understanding.
All homework must be formatted with a title page and submitted in class at the beginning of the period
on the due date. To be eligible for credit, solutions must demonstrate clear and logical reasoning. Late
submissions will be penalized and are only accepted with prior approval or an official, verifiable
excuse. Homework questions will often require you to propose a solution strategy, justify your
assumptions, and compare alternative approaches. The goal is to develop your engineering judgment.
Quizzes will be designed to give you rapid feedback on how well you understand the prior week’s
topics. Each quiz will last for 30 minutes covering the materials discussed in class and assigned in the
practice work. Unless stated otherwise, all quizzes are closed-book. The simple and most used
expressions need to be known and memorized. All other formulae will be provided. The date of the
quiz will be announced beforehand for the main ones. Couple of pop-up quizzes will be given without
prior notice in the first 10 minutes of the class. Quiz questions may require you to propose a solution
strategy, justify your assumptions, and compare alternative approaches.
Lectures, quizzes and homework are expected to be attended/submitted at the scheduled date and on
time. Any arrangements regarding examinations due to special emergency situations or authorized
university activities must be made in advance with the instructor. Missing a midterm or final exam
needs an official excuse.

Mini-Project is a significant component of this course, designed to help student integrate and apply
thermodynamic principles to a realistic, open-ended engineering problem. The project will proceed in
phases, beginning with system definition and first-law analysis, progressing to real fluid and second-
law evaluation, and culminating in a professional presentation. Students may work in teams of 3–4,
with collaboration expected within your team, though each member must contribute meaningfully to
all phases. The presentation should include an executive summary, system description, analysis,
results, and conclusions. The project is worth 5% of your final grade and will be assessed on technical
accuracy, critical analysis, clarity, and completeness. All work must adhere to academic integrity
standards; while discussion of approaches is encouraged, sharing detailed calculations is prohibited.
Dedicated project office hours will be available for assistance throughout the process.
Exams: There will be one/two midterm exam(s) during the semester and a comprehensive final exam.
The date of the exam will be determined by the Registration Office. Exams will be held on a different
location. All Exams–including the final–will be closed-book and any allowed material will be announced
in advance. The final examination will cover all the materials of the course.
Exam re-evaluation: If a student believes that there might be an error in grading a quiz or an exam,
he/she should see the instructor within one (1) week after the assignment/quiz or exam is returned. It
should be noted that the entire exam will be re-graded not just the problem in question, in order to ensure
consistence and fairness. Final course grades may only be contested by following the approved academic
appeal procedures.
A make-up quiz will be allowed under extenuating circumstance, such as hospitalization or bereavement.
Even in these situations, students should realize that the instructor has the right of not to accept an
authenticated excuse. The excuse must be authenticated by Student Success Department and brought to
the instructor immediately (within a week) to qualify for permission to make up any quiz missed.
Repeated quizzes are normally more challenging than the ones that are already given.
Class participation worth 5% of the final grade, is assessed based on your consistent and constructive
engagement during lectures and tutorials, reflecting the professional discourse expected in engineering.
This grade evaluates the quality of student intellectual contribution, which includes asking clarifying
questions to resolve thermodynamic concepts, volunteering answers to conceptual prompts, engaging
in problem-solving at the board during tutorials, and contributing meaningfully to peer discussions. To
earn credit, you must demonstrate prepared and thoughtful interaction; merely attending class without
active engagement will result in a minimal score. Please note that adherence to the attendance policy is
fundamental, as absences will inherently limit your opportunity to contribute and will affect this
portion of your grade.
Attendance is mandatory for this course. The curriculum is cumulative, and missing class creates
significant gaps in understanding that are difficult to overcome. Attendance will be recorded at every
meeting and reported to the University Registrar. In general,

▪ Arriving late disrupts the learning environment. Three instances of lateness will be recorded as
one unexcused absence. Arriving more than 15 minutes after the start of class will be recorded
as a full absence.
▪ Leaving class during lecture is disruptive and strongly discouraged except of emergency.

TEACHING PLAN (Lecture):
Week 1 Chapter 1: General introduction to chemical engineering thermodynamics. Define units of
pressure, temperature, density, mass, and moles, SI and English system, and conversion of
units.
Week 2 Chapter 2: First Law of Thermodynamics: Introducing relevant concepts and terminology,
e.g. internal energy, potential energy, kinetic energy, specific volume, heat capacity, heat,
work, enthalpy, state and state functions.
Week 3 Energy balance for a closed system: Applications
Week 4 Analysis of open systems: Mass and Energy Balances.
Week 5 Open systems: Selected applications, e.g. turbines, nozzle, etc.
Week 6 Chapter3: Volumetric properties of pure fluids: PVT behavior of pure substances, ideal
gas and equations of state (EoS)
Week 7 Cubic equations of state, theorem of corresponding states, generalized correlations for
gases, generalized correlations for liquids.
Week 8 Chapter 4: Heat Effects: Sensible heat effects, latent heat of pure substances.
o o
Week 9 Standard heat of reaction ( H), temperature dependence of  H, heat effects of reactions.
Week 10 Chapter 5: Second law of thermodynamics: Introduction, heat engines, Carnot theorem
Week 11 Second law of thermodynamics: Entropy, entropy change of an ideal gas, mathematical
statement of the second law, Entropy balance for open systems.
Week 12 Second law of thermodynamics: Ideal work, lost work, third law of thermodynamics.
Week 13 Chapter 6: Thermodynamic Properties of fluids: Property relations for homogenous phases
Week 14 Thermodynamic Properties of fluids: Residual properties, two phase systems, generalized
property correlations for gases
Week 15 Chapter 7: Selected applications of thermodynamics to flow processes.
