MATH 251 Operations Research I
(4 Lecture – 4 Credits)
Prerequisites: MATH 204 or MATH 211
Course Catalog Description:
This course introduces operations research and deterministic mathematical modeling with a focus
on linear programming. Topics include graphical interpretation of linear optimization problems,
simplex method, duality, sensitivity analysis, and general solution strategies. Emphasis is given to
modeling industrial problems and interpretation of the solutions obtained. Students learn to use
modern modeling languages and software to find optimal solutions for large-scale problems.

Course Topics
1. Introduction to operations research and linear programming (5 lectures)
2. Linear optimization modeling: components of optimization models (6 lectures)
3. Linear optimization modeling of real-world problems I (6 lectures)
4. Implement linear optimization models and interpret results (6 lectures)
5. The simplex method (6 lectures)
6. The big M method, two-phase simplex method (4 lectures)
7. The revised simplex method (5 lectures)
8. Duality and sensitivity analysis (6 lectures)
9. Dual simplex method (4 lectures)
10. Transportation and assignment models (6 lectures)
11. Network optimization models (shortest path problem, max flow, min cost) (6 lectures)
Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Lectures are either 2 x 75 and 1 x 50 minutes or 2 x 100 minutes per week. Lectures are presented
using the whiteboard, and may be complemented with handouts and/or PowerPoint slides. The
lectures include problem solving and tutorials with use of software packages. Students work in group
projects to solve and analyze optimization problems.
Software Resources:
LINDO/LINGO, AMPL CPLEX, GUROBI, AIMMS, EXCEL.

Additional Information:
Assessment
There are 4 quizzes scheduled in this course. Each quiz will therefore count for 3.75% of your
final grade for the course.
Project
The project will be assigned in the week 6 of the semester. Students will work in groups of 3 to 4
on a real-world optimization problem from economics, engineering or social sciences. Students
can choose to use any optimization software available (LINGO, AMPL, GAMS, AIMMS,
GUROBI, etc.). Students will submit a written report containing case description, solution
methodology, code, and discussion of the obtained results. Any late submission will be penalized
by 10% of the assessment grade per day.
Mobile phone Policy
Mobile phones may not be used as calculators in the Quizzes, Semester Examination or Final
Examination. Mobile phones must be switched off during quizzes and exams and must be put
away for the duration of the quiz/exam.
Calculator Policy
You may use a basic scientific calculator to help you with arithmetic calculations.
Calculators with any sort of graphing, symbolic manipulation or integral and derivative
calculation capabilities are strictly prohibited. Any such device found with a student during
any form of assessment will be treated as a violation of the above-stated Honor Code Academic
Pledge.
