CHEG 350 Materials Science & Engineering
(3 lectures – 3 credits)
Prerequisites: CHEM 116, PHYS 122
Course Catalog Description:
Introduction to materials science and engineering. Metals, alloys, ceramics, polymers, and
composites; inter-atomic bonding, crystal structure and defects; diffusion, nucleation and
microstructure; phase diagrams and phase transformations; mechanical properties; material
failure; corrosion and degradation.
Textbook:
Materials Science and Engineering: An Introduction by William D. Callister, Publisher: Wiley,
th
9 Intl Edition, 2014, ISBN-13: 9781118319222
Reference Materials:
th
Introduction to Materials Science for Engineers by James F. Shackelford, Prentice Hall, 8 Ed.,
2015.
th
Foundations of Materials Science and Engineering by William F. Smith, McGraw-Hill, 5 Ed.,
2010.
Course Topics:
1. Introduction. Atomic structure and interatomic bonding. (4 lectures)
2. The structure of crystalline solids (4 lectures)
3. Imperfections in solids (3 lectures)
4. Diffusion (3 lectures)
5. Mechanical properties of metals (4 lectures)
6. Dislocations and strengthening mechanisms. (3 lectures)
7. Failure: fracture, fatigue, creep (4 lectures)
8. Phase diagrams (4 lectures)
9. Phase transformations(4 lectures)
10. Ceramic materials (4 lectures)
11. Polymers and Composite materials (4 lectures)
12. Corrosion and electrochemistry. (4 lectures)
Structure and learning methodologies:
The course will be delivered through three 50-min PowerPoint-enhanced lectures per week.
Homework problems/questions will be given regularly to help students elaborate the subjects
taught, and are considered as assigned homework every student should work out and deliver.
On average, one-week time will be available for each homework assignment that the students
should distribute evenly in order to work on and deliver the solutions to the given problems on
time. Homework solutions should be neat and stapled. Five quizzes, one midterm exam, and
one final exam will be given. The grade distribution of the given assessment tools is shown in
the below table.
Assessment:
Coursework (homework, participation/in-class activities) 20%
Quizzes 30%
Midterm Exam 20%
Final Exam 30%
