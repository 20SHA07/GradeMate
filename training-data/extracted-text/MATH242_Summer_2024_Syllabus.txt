MATH 242 Introduction to Probability and Statistics
(3 Lectures, 0 Laboratory, 0 Tutorials – 3 Credits)
Prerequisite: MATH 112
Course Catalog Description:
This course introduces students to basic probability models and statistical methods for data analysis.
The course will cover introductory probability theory, discrete and continuous probability
distributions, elements of descriptive statistics, and different statistical inference methods such as
estimation for the mean and the variance, hypothesis testing for the mean and the variance.

Course Topics:
1. Introduction to Probability: Sample spaces, axioms of probability and counting methods (4
lectures)
2. Conditional probability: Law of total probability, Bayes’ theorem and independent events (3
lectures)
3. Discrete and continuous random variables: probability mass functions, probability density
functions, cumulative distribution functions and their use in computing probabilities (4 lectures)
4. Two-dimensional random vectors, joint and marginal distributions (3 lectures)
5. Independent random variables and the notion of a random sample (1 lecture)
6. Expectation, variance and median of a random variable (3 lectures)
7. Covariance and correlation (2 lectures)
8. Common discrete probability distributions: Bernoulli, Binomial and Poisson (3 lectures)
9. Common continuous probability distributions: Normal and Gamma distributions (3 lectures)
10. Large Sample Theory: the law of large numbers, the central limit theorem and precision
computations (4 lectures)
11. Elements of descriptive statistics: frequencies, tables and graphs (2 lectures)
12. Sampling distributions (I): Sample Mean, Sample Variance and their distributions when
sampling from Normal distributions (2 lectures)
13. Sampling distributions (II): The chi-squared, the t distribution (2 lectures)
14. Confidence intervals for the mean based on small normal samples or large arbitrary samples (3
lectures)
15. Confidence intervals for a proportion based on large arbitrary samples (2 lectures)
16. Hypotheses testing: t-tests of the mean, tests of a proportion, tests of the variance (4 lectures)
Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Lectures will be either 3 x 50 minutes or 2 x 75 minutes per week. Lectures will be presented using
the whiteboard, and may be complemented with handouts and/or PowerPoint slides.

submitted late, the project grade will be decreased by 10% for each day of delay.
