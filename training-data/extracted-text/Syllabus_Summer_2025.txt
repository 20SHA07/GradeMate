PHYS 121 University Physics I
(3 Lectures, 2 Laboratory, 1 Tutorial – 4 Credits)
Prerequisite: MATH 111
Course Catalog Description:
This course gives a vector-based and calculus-based introduction to fundamental concepts in
Newtonian mechanics, mechanical conservation laws, oscillations and waves. The course includes
laboratory activities with experiments that demonstrate key concepts discussed in the lectures.
Textbook:
Physics for Scientists and Engineers - Volume 1 by Raymond A. Serway and John W Jewett,
Publisher: Brooks Cole, 9th International Edition, 2014. ISBN-13: 9781285070438 (ISBN-
10:1285070437).
Course Topics:
1. Basic Concepts: Fundamental and derived units, dimensions. (3 lectures)
2. Force and Motion: Velocity, acceleration, and relative motion, Newton’s Laws, friction. (5
lectures)
3. Gravitation: Weight, mass, other types of forces. (2 lectures)
4. Systems of Particles: Centre of mass, linear momentum. (4 lectures)
5. Equilibrium: Requirements, center of gravity, elasticity, elastic moduli. (3 lectures)
6. Work and Energy: Constant and variable force, kinetic and potential energy, conservation of
energy. (4 lectures)
7. Collisions: Elastic and inelastic, impulse, and center of mass. (4 lectures)
8. Rotational Motion: Angular kinematics. (5 lectures)
9. Rotational Dynamics: Torque, Newton’s Laws, angular momentum. (6 lectures)
10. Circular Motion: Newton’s Second Law Applied to uniform circular motion, motion in
accelerated frames. (3 lectures)
11. Oscillations: SHM, resonance, energy considerations. (4 lectures)
12. Mechanical waves. (2 lectures)
Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Lectures will be 3 × 50 minutes, or 2 × 75 minutes, per week. Tutorials will be 1 × 50 minutes per
week. Laboratory will be 1 × 100 minutes per week. Lectures will be presented using both
PowerPoint slides and the whiteboard; copies of PowerPoint slides will be made available to
students.
Laboratory Experiments:
A minimum of 10 laboratory experiments will be conducted from the following list of topics
according to the material covered in the lectures:
1. Units, Measurements and Graphs.
2. Uniformly Accelerated Motion I.
3. Uniformly Accelerated Motion II.
4. Coefficient of Friction.
5. Inelastic Collisions.
6. Elastic Collisions.
7. Force and Torque.
8. Energy Measurements.
9. Centrifugal Force.
10. Hooke’s Law.
11. Simple Harmonic Motion.

Instructor Policy on Late Submission of Assignments:
• Quizzes: There will be four quizzes on the given date and during the start of the classes i.e. from 11:00 am
to 11:30 am. The grades of all quizzes will be considered for the average, and there will be no makeup
quiz, no extension, or time change for any quiz. If you missed a quiz, it is your responsibility to contact
the student success office and provide them with a legitimate excuse (medical note, for example). You need
to arrange for an email to be sent to the professor (by the student success office). Only then will the missing
quiz be assigned a grade equal to the average of all other quizzes.
• HW: The WebAssign platform will be used for HWs. For each HW, you will be given ample time to finish
it, and there will be no extension or time change for HW. It is important to solve the HW on your own,
without getting help from outside sources. This is an important way to get you ready for the other online
tests.
• Midterm (MT): The MT exam will be on campus and will be scheduled by the registration office. There
will be no make-up MT. The MT test will cover the first seven chapters (Chap 1 to 7). If you missed this
exam and you have a valid excuse, the final exam will carry all the point weight of the combined midterm
and the final exam (e.g., 50%).
• Final Exam: Final Exam will be on-campus and will be scheduled by the registration office.
