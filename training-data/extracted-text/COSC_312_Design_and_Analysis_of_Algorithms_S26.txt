COSC 312 Design and Analysis of Algorithms
(2 Lecture, 2 Tutorial – 3 Credits)
Prerequisite: COSC 301, COSC 310
Course Catalog Description:
This course covers the most important algorithm strategies and solution techniques,
independent of programming language or computer hardware. Topics include: Big-O notation;
worst and average case analysis; recurrences and asymptotics; efficient algorithms for sorting,
searching, and selection; algorithm design techniques: divide-and-conquer, dynamic
programming, greedy algorithms, randomization; algorithms for fundamental graph problems;
string algorithms; and numerical methods.
Textbook:
• Thomas Cormen, Charles E. Leiserson, Ronald L. Rivest, and Clifford Stein, Introduction
to Algorithms, MIT Press, 4th Edition, 2022. ISBN: 978-0262046305.
Reference Material:
• Jon Kleinberg and Éva Tardos, Algorithm Design, Pearson, 2005. ISBN: 978-0321295354.
• Magnus Lie Hetland, Python Algorithms: Mastering Basic Algorithms in the Python
Language (Expert's Voice in Open Source), Apress, 1st Edition, 2010. ISBN: 978-
1430232377.
• Uri M. Ascher, and Chen Greif, A First Course in Numerical Methods, SIAM
Computational Science and Engineering, 2011. ISBN: 978-0898719970.
Course Structure:
The course is delivered in form of classroom lectures and problem solving sessions. The
lectures will be 2 × 50-min lectures. Tutorial sessions will also be 2 × 50-min per week.
Individual assignments and quizzes are held throughout the semester to reinforce knowledge
of the material.
Course Topics:
1. Introduction: searching and sorting, analyzing algorithms, designing algorithms. (2
lectures, week 1)
2. Algorithm analysis: algorithm correctness, inductive proofs and loop invariants, asymptotic
notation, worst case analysis, recurrences, the substitution method, recursion tree, the
Master theorem. (4 lectures, weeks 2, 3)
3. Divide-and-conquer: the maximum- subarray problem, Strassen’s matrix multiplication
algorithm. (4 lectures, weeks 4, 5)
4. Sorting in linear time: lower bounds for sorting, counting sort, radix sort, bucket sort (2
lectures, week 6)
5. Randomized algorithms: quicksort, median finding. (2 lectures, week 7)
6. Algorithmic strategies for optimization: brute-force, greedy, branch-and-bound, dynamic
programming. (4 lectures, weeks 8, 9)
7. Graph algorithms: spanning trees, shortest paths, network flows. (6 lectures, weeks 10-12)
8. String algorithms: Pattern matching and string algorithms, regular expressions, finite state
machines. (4 lectures, weeks 13, 14)
9. Numerical Algorithms: Bisection method, Newton's method, one-dimensional
optimization. (2 lectures, week 15)
