BMED 351 Biomedical Circuits and Systems
(3 Lectures, 3 laboratories – 4 Credits)
Prerequisites: PHYS 122, BMED 212
Course Catalog Description:
The primary objective of this course is to study analogue, digital electronic circuits and their application
to biomedical instrumentation and physiological measurements. The course will focus strongly on
electronic hardware and software design issues required to produce medical instruments, which satisfy
International standards for safety, performance and quality control. Students will be equipped with the
fundamental knowledge required to design Biosignal processing system.

Course topics:
1. Electronic circuit elements (resistor, capacitor, inductor, diode, transistor (6 lectures)
2. Ohm’s law, Kirchhoff’s current and voltage laws (8 lectures)
3. Thevenin’s and superposition Theorem for circuit analysis (6 lectures)
4. Transient and steady state analysis of ele ctronic circuit (3 lectures)
5. Operational amplifier and instrumentation amplifier (6 lectures)
6. Design of simple passive and active analogue filters (3 lectures)
7. Signal sampling theory, analogue to digital converter and digital to analogue converter. (5 lectures)
8. Basic Concepts of electronic circuits for safety systems in medical instrumentation Action
Potentials of cells and Bio-potential Electrodes (2 lectures)
9. Biomedical sensor electronics and sensor calibration (2 lectures)
10. Microcomputer system in biomedical devices (4 lectures)
Laboratory experiments:
1) Introduction to Biomedical Circuits Experiment Instruments and circuit components
2) Design and implementation of a simple circuit to demonstrate Kirchoff’s current and voltage laws
3) Design and implementation of a simple circuit to demonstrate Thevenin’s and Superposition
theorem
4) Design and implementation of a practical operational amplifier to amplify and add the two
sinusoidal signals.
5) Demonstration of relationship between voltage and current in diode and transistor
6) Design and implementation of active and passive analogue filters (low pass and high pass)
7) Design of a simple data acquisition system using a microcontroller system by applying the sampling
theorem.
8) Demonstrate the measurement of bio-potentials (heart Electrocardiogram, muscles
Electromyogram, brain Electroencephalogram) through e-health data acquisition system.
9) Demonstrate the measurement of biomedical signals by using biomedical sensors (pressure
transducers for Blood Pressure and Respiratory flow spirometry, condenser microphone for heart

Structure and learning methodologies:
Lectures will be 2x75 minutes and 1x150 minutes laboratory per week. Learning methodology includes

learning.
