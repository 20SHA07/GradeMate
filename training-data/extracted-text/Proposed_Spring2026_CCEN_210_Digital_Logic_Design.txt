CCEN 210: Digital Logic Design
(3 Lecture hours, 3 Laboratory/Studio hours, 4 Credits)
Prerequisites
• COSC 114 Introduction to Computing- Python
Course Catalog Description
This course covers the principles of digital logic design, including data representation in digital systems, Boolean
algebra, and logic function implementation. It introduces combinational and sequential logic design, computer-aided
design (CAD) tools, hardware description languages, and the use of artificial intelligence (AI) methods for digital logic
circuit design and optimization. Sustainability is emphasized through circuit minimization. Laboratory experiments
provide hands-on experience with simulating, implementing, and testing digital circuits.

Course Topics
Week # Topics
01 Numbering Systems: Introduction to digital logic, numbering systems (decimal, binary, hexadecimal,
octal), and conversion between these systems. Basic mathematical operations in the binary system.
02 Boolean Algebra: Introduction to Boolean algebra, logic operations and their mapping to logic gates
(e.g., AND, OR, INV, XOR).
03 Introduce truth tables and describe their functionality. Synthesize Boolean functions. Construct truth
tables and derive Boolean functions using Product of Sum (POS) and Sum of Products (SOP).
04 Logic Minimization: Apply Boolean algebra to minimize logic.
05 Perform minimization using Karnaugh maps with emphasis on sustainability through area, power, and
latency optimization. Use NAND and NOR gates to synthesize logic expressions.
06 Introduction to Computer-Aided Design (CAD) tools, background of hardware description languages
(HDLs), and basic concepts of Verilog, including gate-level primitives, modules, ports, structural and
behavioral descriptions, continuous assignments, and procedural statements.
07 Arithmetic Logic: Design circuits for arithmetic operations (e.g., half adders, full adders, subtractors),
analyze performance issues in large circuits, and use Verilog to specify arithmetic circuits.
08 Introduce other combinational components such as multipliers, encoders, decoders, and multiplexers,
and demonstrate how to implement them using Verilog.
09 Sequential Logic Design: asynchronous vs. synchronous operations, latch and flip-flops (FFs), edge-
triggered vs. level sensitive FF. (state diagram, state table, state assignment and circuit synthesis)
10 Explain logic circuit blocks that store information, understand the design and operation of various flip-
flops, and analyze the major timing parameters that affect flip-flop operation.
11 Registers and counters. Verilog for sequential elements and timing, and an introduction to synchronous
sequential state machines.
12 Design finite state machines (FSMs) using Moore and Mealy models, analyze the impact of state

TEACHING PLAN (Lecture):
Week No Contents
Week 1 Introduction digital logic. Numbering Systems (decimal, binary, hex, octal) and conversion
between the systems. Basic mathematical operation in the binary system.
Week 2 Introduction to Boolean Algebra, logic operations and mapping to logic gates (AND, OR, INV,
XOR). Introduce truth table and describe its functionality. Synthesis Boolean function
Week 3 Set-up the truth table and derive the Boolean function of a logic circuit using Product of Sum
(POS) and Sum of Products (SOP). Use NAND and NOR gates to synthesis logic expressions.
Week 4 Use Boolean Algebra to minimize logic. Obtain the minimum SOP or POS. Use don’t care
condition.
Week 5 Use K-map for 3-6 variables. Given a problem statement or function Build correct K-map and
synthesis logic from K-map.
Week 6 Introduced to Computer Aided Design (CAD) Tools. Get familiar with background of HDLs, Basic
concepts of Verilog languages, Gate level primitives, Module, Ports, Structural and behavioral
descriptions,
Continuous assignment and procedural statements
Week 7 Arithmetic Logic. Design circuits used to perform arithmetic
operations, Understand performance issues in large circuits, Use Verilog to specify arithmetic
circuits
Week 8 Mux and decode logic. Define Mux and decode logic stand alone and to build any function.
Week 9 Introducing Sequential element (latch, flip-flop). Explain the difference between combinational
and sequential logic circuits
Week 10 Explain the operation and design of registers, counters.
Introduce the key timing parameters that affect flip-flop performance.
Week 11 Verilog for sequential elements and timing
Introducing synchronous sequential state machine
Week 12 Design FSM using Moore and Mealy. Impact of state assignment on implementation. Write
Verilog for FSM
Week 13 Introduce programmable logic devices, including Programmable Array Logic (PAL),
Programmable Logic Array (PLA), and Field Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGA), and explain their
applications in digital system design.
Week 14 Introduce applications of AI in digital design, including circuit optimization, circuit generation
(e.g., Verilog), and automated test bench creation.
