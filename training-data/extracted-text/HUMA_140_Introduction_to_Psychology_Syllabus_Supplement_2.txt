HUMA 140 Introduction to Psychology
(3 Lectures, 0 Laboratory – 3 Credits)
Pre-requisite: ENGL 101 or ENGL 111
Course Catalog Description:
This course examines historical and current topics related to the mind and behavior with research methods
and the scientific method as a foundation. Areas covered in this course include biology, development,
memory, learning, social psychology, personality, psychological disorders, health psychology, and
positive psychology.

Course Topics:
1. Introduction to psychology: contemporary psychology, early research, pioneers (3 lectures)
2. Research methodologies: planning and conducting research, analyzing data, reporting results, and
research ethics (4 lectures)
3. The biology of the mind and consciousness: neuroanatomy and neurophysiology, brain areas, the
endocrine system, and stages of sleep (6 lectures)
4. Development through the lifespan: genes, chromosomes, inheritance of behavior, epigenetics,
continuity and stage theory, and stability and change in behavior (3 lectures)
5. Learning: classical conditioning, operant conditioning, evolutionary influences on behavior, and
observational learning (5 lectures)
6. Memory: the information-processing model; encoding, storage, and retrieval of memories,
forgetting, memory construction errors, and strategies to improve memory (4 lectures)
7. Motivation and emotion: physiology of hunger and motivational concepts such as drives, incentives,
arousal theory, and Maslow’s hierarchy of needs (1 lecture)
8. Stress, health psychology, and positive psychology: stressors, stress reactions, the immune system,
stress management techniques, coping with stress, and positive psychology interventions to improve
overall life satisfaction (6 lectures)
9. Social psychology: social thinking, attitudes versus behavior, conformity, groupthink, aggression,
and altruism (3 lectures)
10. Personality: psychodynamic theories, new advances in neuroscience, biological basis for personality
traits, social-cognitive theories, and self-esteem (4 lectures)
11. Psychological disorders: psychological disorders such as anxiety, substance use disorders, major
depressive and bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, eating disorders, and anti-social personality disorders
(6 lectures)

Description of the Assessments
Quizzes – (10% each)
Three quizzes worth 10% each are required for this course. Each quiz is 25 questions and will test recall
and recognition of the material studied. The first quiz covers material from introduction to psychology to
development through the lifespan. The second quiz covers material from learning to social psychology.
The third quiz covers material from personality to psychological disorders. Each quiz will consist of 25
questions.

Research Assignment (10%)
Students are required to undertake the Gosling et al. (2003) Ten Item Personality Inventory (TIPI) which
is a short measure for the Costa and McCrae (1992) Five Factor Inventory for personality traits. Students
will discuss their results and use the literature to discuss whether their personality traits align to music,
language, and other personal preferences in social situations. Using the literature, students will be required
to estimate whether the TIPI is valid and reliable. The students will be required provide critical analysis
as to whether there exists a neurobiological basis that explains personality traits. Students are required to
obtain a minimum of three peer reviewed journal citations. All references must be IEEE compliant.
Midterm Exam (30%)
This is a cumulative in-class exam held halfway through the academic semester. The exam will consist
of three essay questions worth 75% as well as 25 fill-in-the-blank and multiple choice questions worth
25%.
Final Project (30%)
Students will provide an in-class poster presentation on a selected topic. Students will devise a research
topic based on the topics studied in the course and seek approval of the research topic form the course
leader. Once selected, the research topic is not subject to change. Students will need to use a section
highlighting the research methods used for the poster. Students need to conduct a professional
presentation about the topic using at least five peer reviewed journal citations. All references must be
IEEE compliant.
