MATH 244 Probability
(3 Lecture – 3 Credits)
Prerequisite: MATH 101, MATH 112
Course Catalog Description:
This course introduces the mathematical theory of probability at an undergraduate level of rigor. The course
covers basic concepts of axiomatic probability and conditional probability, random variables/vectors and their
distribution, moments, and various models of random variables. Students also study classical probability
inequalities and limit theorems in large sample theory.

Course Topics:
1. Introduction to set theory and counting (2 lectures)
2. Probability axioms and simple probability spaces (4 lectures)
3. Conditional probability and independence (3 lectures)
4. Random variables, random vectors and their distributions (6 lectures)
5. Marginal and conditional distributions (4 lectures)
6. Laws of functions of one random variable (2 lectures)
7. Laws of linear combinations of two independent random variables (2 lectures)
8. Laws of the maximum and the minimum for i.i.d. random variables (2 lectures)
9. Expectation, variance and higher order moments; expectation inequalities (4 lectures)
10. Moment generating functions (2 lectures)
11. Common families of distributions (3 lectures)
12. Probability concentration inequalities (2 lectures)
13. Convergence in probability and convergence in distribution (3 lectures)
14. Laws of large numbers, classical central limit theorem and the delta-method (5 lectures)
15. The sample mean and the sample variance: characteristics and distributions (1 lecture)
Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Lectures are either 3 x 50-minutes or 2 x 75 minutes per week. Lectures will be presented using the whiteboard,
and may be complemented with handouts or presentation slides.
