MATH 101 Fundamentals of Mathematical Reasoning
(3 Lecture – 3 Credits)
Prerequisite: None
Course Catalog Description:
This course provides a foundation in logical and mathematical reasoning. It develops first year university
students’ structured logical thinking and mathematical rigor. The course introduces methods of proof,
basic concepts and properties of real numbers, relations and functions. The course also presents an
introduction to combinatorics, set theory and number theory.
Textbook:
• Mathematical Reasoning Writing and Proof by Ted Sundstrom, Open Textbook Initiative, American
Institute of Mathematics, Version 2.1, 2021
Reference Materials:
• Mathematical Proofs: A Transition to Advanced Mathematics by Gary Chartrand,
th
Albert D. Polimeni, Ping Zhang, Publisher: Pearson, 4 Edition, 2017, ISBN: 9780134746753.
Course Topics:
Numbers (1 lecture)
• Introduction to different types of numbers: natural, integers, rational, irrational, real.
Logic I (4 lectures)
• Propositional calculus: truth tables, tautology, contradiction, negation.
Logic II (4 lectures)
• Predicate calculus: implication, equivalence, existential and universal quantifiers.
Methods of Proof (6 lectures)
• Direct method: basic proofs and examples.
• Contrapositive: understanding and applying contrapositive proofs.
• Contradiction: proofs by contradiction and its applications.
Mathematical Induction (3 lectures)
• Induction: principle of mathematical induction, strong induction.
Set Theory Operations on sets (6 lectures)
• Basics of set theory: definition of sets, subset, cardinality.
• Operations on sets: union, intersection, difference, complement.
• Venn diagrams: representing relationships between sets.
Set Theory Properties (3 lectures)
• De Morgan’s Laws: understanding and applying De Morgan's Laws.
• Infinitude of primes: proof and implications.

Functions (6 lectures)
• Introduction to relations and functions.
• Injectivity, surjectivity, bijectivity: definitions and examples.
• Composition and inverse of functions.
Number Theory (6 lectures)
• Prime numbers: definition, properties, distribution.
• Divisibility and prime decomposition: fundamental theorem of arithmetic.
• Greatest common divisor (GCD) and least common multiple (LCM).
• Change of base and operations in bases other than 10.
Basic Combinatorics (6 lectures)
• Counting rules: multiplication principle, addition principle.
• Permutations: arrangements, permutations with repetition.
• Combinations: selection, combinations with repetition.
• Binomial theorem: expansion and applications.
Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Lectures are either 3 x 50 minutes per week or 2 x 75 minutes per week. Lectures will be presented
using the whiteboard, and may be complemented with handouts and/or PowerPoint slides. Students will
work in groups on a project related to the course topics.
