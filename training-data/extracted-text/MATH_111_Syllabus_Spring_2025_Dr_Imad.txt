Course Catalog Description: This course will introduce students to the theory and techniques of single
variable differential and integral calculus. The emphasis is on problem solving in a science and engineering
context, as opposed to theory. Topics include studying the exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric, and
polynomial functions and their limits, continuity, derivatives, extrema, integration, area under a curve, and
volumes of revolution.
Textbook: Calculus: Early Transcendental Functions by Robert T. Smith, Roland B. Minton and Ziad A. T. Rafhi,
McGraw-Hill, 5th Edition, 2018. ISBN: 9780077166472.
Reference Materials: Calculus, James Stewart, Publisher: CENGAGE Learning, 8th Edition (Metric Version),
2016. ISBN: 9781305266728. Calculus: Concepts & Connections, 1st Edition, by Robert Smith and Roland
Minton, McGraw Hill, 2007. ISBN: 9780073309293.

Course Learning Outcomes
1 Solve problems involving limits and continuity.
2 Define the derivative of a function and apply the techniques of differentiation.
Combine knowledge of limits, continuity and derivatives in order to classify the important characteristics of
3
a function and draw a graph of the function which illustrates all these characteristics.
4 Apply the techniques of differentiation to solve optimization and related rates problems.
5 Define indefinite integrals and definite integrals and apply them to solving area and volume problems.

MAKE UP POLICY
Quizzes • There will be no make -up quizzes. If the student fails to attend a scheduled quiz without an approved
absence from the Registrar’s Office, then a grade of zero for that quiz will be assigned.
• If the student fails to attend a quiz and the Registrar’s Office confirms that the student's absence from that

Midterm Examination • There will be no make -up Midterm Examination. If the student fails to attend the
scheduled Midterm Examination without an approved absence from the Registrar’s Office, then a grade of zero
will be assigned for the Midterm Examination.
• If the student fails to attend the Midterm Examination and the Registrar’s Office confirms that the absence
from the Midterm Examination is approved, then the grade for the Final Examination will also be assigned as the
grade for the Midterm Examination.
A maximum of 40% of the course grade can be compensated through the above policy.

Additional Information
Assessments
There are four quizzes scheduled for this course, counting for 20% of the final grade. Homework on
Connect will count for 6%, Tutorial classes count for 9%, Pebble for 5% Midterm for 25% and Final
Exam for 35%.
Calculator Policy
Only basic non-programmable non-graphical calculators are allowed during quizzes and exams.
On campus Classroom Policies
Classes start on time. Students arriving late to class are a distraction to both the instructor and other
students and will therefore be marked absent.
Students are expected to bring for each class what they need for an effective lecture: lecture notes
(posted on Blackboard), pen/pencil, notebook, calculator etc.
Expectations from students
To succeed well in this course, students are required to work at least one hour outside the classroom
for each hour spent in class. That means four hours a week, every week. This is an average
requirement for an average student with a sufficient background. It therefore could be more if some
topics from previous courses are not known well enough.
Selected problems for each section are recommended as homework. Homework will not be collected,
but are essential part of the learning process, and are therefore recommended.
Grading
Writing correct solutions is essential in the life of a scientist, including the correct use of the
mathematical symbols. Not only one needs to be understood by others, but only by paying attention
to what is written a student can acquire a good understanding of the concepts. Therefore, in all
assessments, the correctness of the solution writing will be evaluated and marks will be deducted
for mistakes (notations, clarity, structure of the solution, etc)
For quizzes and midterm, students have TWO DAYS after they get back their copy to check if there are
any grading mistakes, and if so, ask for revision. After two days, the result will be considered final and
no more revision will be allowed.
Conflict Resolution Procedure
For any issue, the following procedure needs to be followed:
1) students need to talk first to their instructor;
2) if (1) did not resolve the issue, students can talk to the course coordinator;
3) if (1) and (2) did not resolve the issue, students can talk to the chair of the math department;
4) if none of the above resolved the issue, students can talk to the Associate dean of undergraduate
studies.
5) No Grade Appeal is approved if the student has not seen the copy of his/her final exam.
There will be no leniency with phones and any other electronics during exams.
