MATH 234 Discrete Mathematics
(3 Lectures, 0 Laboratory – 3 Credits)
Prerequisite: MATH 112
Co-requisite: None
Course Catalog Description:
Topics covered in this course include propositional and predicate calculus, mathematical reasoning
including mathematical induction, an introduction to sets, basic number theory, functions, relations,
graphs, trees, cardinality, counting techniques, linear recurrence relations, and Boolean Algebra.
Textbook:

Course Topics:
1. Propositional and predicate calculus. (4 lectures)
2. Mathematical reasoning and mathematical induction. (6 lectures)
3. Sets, functions, and cardinality. (4 lectures)
4. Counting techniques. (6 lectures)
5. Relations, graphs and trees. (8 lectures)
6. Basic number theory. (6 lectures)
7. Linear recurrence relations. (6 lectures)
8. Boolean Algebra. (5 lectures)
Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Lectures will either be 3 x 50 minutes or 2 x 75 minutes per week. Lectures will be presented using the
whiteboard, and may be complemented with handouts and/or PowerPoint slides.
Laboratory Experiment Topics: Not applicable.
Laboratory and/or Computer Resources: Not applicable.

Calculator Policy
You may use a basic scientific calculator to help you with arithmetic computations in the MATH-206
course. The calculator must not have a graphing capability. The calculator must not be capable of
performing symbolic manipulation. The calculator must not be capable of computing derivatives and
integrals. A good example of an appropriate calculator is the Casio fx-82ES.
You will be permitted to use your (basic) calculator in the Quizzes, Midterm Examination and the
Final Examination. Please do not try to use a more powerful calculator in the Quizzes and
Examinations as it will be confiscated.
Mobile phones may not be used as calculators in the Quizzes, Midterm Examination or Final
Examination. Mobile phones must be switched off during quizzes and exams and must be put away
for the duration of the quiz/exam.
Classroom Policies
Discipline is one of the main components of your academic and professional success.
Please make every effort to arrive at the class periods in a timely fashion. Students arriving late to
class are a distraction to both the instructor and the other students in the online classroom. If students
require the class periods to end on-time then the class periods must begin on-time.

bring a pen or pencil, notepaper, a calculator and a ruler. If all of this equipment makes your bag heavy,
consider leaving your mobile phone at home to lessen the load you have to carry.
