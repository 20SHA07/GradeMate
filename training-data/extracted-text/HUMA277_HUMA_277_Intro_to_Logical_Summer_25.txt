HUMA 277 Introduction to Logical Reasoning 
(3 Lectures, 0 Laboratory - 3 credits) 

Prerequisite: ENGL 102 
Co-requisite: None

Course Catalog Description:
This course provides students with a solid introduction to logical thinking and critical analysis. Emphasis will be placed on arguments as basic units of thinking. By understanding the importance of the validity and soundness of reasoning, students will be able to identify, analyze, and evaluate arguments in scientific language and everyday discourse. 

Textbook: 
A Concise Introduction to Logic by P.J Hurley & Lori Watson, Publisher: Cengage Learning, 13th edition, 2018, ISBN: 9781305959767

Reference Materials: 
The Power of Logic by D. Howard-Snyder, F. Howard-Snyder, R. Wasserman, Publisher: McGraw-Hill, 5th edition, 2012, ISBN: 978-0078038198
An Introduction to Symbolic Logic by Susanne Katherina Langer, Publisher: Kessinger Publishing, 2009, ISBN: 9780486601649

Course Topics: 
Basic concepts: soundness, cogency, premises and conclusions (3 lectures)
Inference: deduction and induction (3 lectures) 
Language and meaning: intention and extension (3 lectures) 
Analogy: legal and moral reasoning (3 lectures) 
Categorical propositions: quality, quantity, and distribution (3 lectures) 
Immediate inference: traditional square of opposition and Venn diagrams (3 lectures)
Fallacies: formal and informal (3 lectures) 
Propositional logic: symbolic logic, symbols, and translation (3 lectures)
Truth functions and truth tables (3 lectures) 
Kinds of logical arguments (2 lectures) 
Natural deduction in propositional logic: rules of implications and replacement (6 lectures)
Theories of probability and probability calculus (3 lectures)
Statistical reasoning (3 lectures) 
The Scientific method of observation, experimentation, generalization, and Mill’s methods of causal inference (4 lectures) 

Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Lectures will be 3 x 50 minutes or 2 x 75 minutes per week. Course delivery focuses on lectures, problem solving, and in-class discussions. These discussions are related to informal logic and particularly fallacies, which occupy three sessions. The course is delivered through lectures, with an emphasis on problem solving and debating contemporary issues employing acquired logical skills.

Instructor Policy on Late Submission of Assignments:
QUIZZES – There will be no make-up quizzes. Without an approved absence, the score is 0 (zero) for a missed quiz.
Semester Exam - There will be no make-up midterm. If you miss the midterm and you have an approved excuse, the final exam will carry all the weight of the combined midterm and the final exam (e.g. 50%). Note that this means missing the midterm without a valid excuse implies having a Zero.
