COSC 301 Automata, Computability, and Complexity
(2 Lecture, 2 Tutorial/Problem-solving – 3 Credits)
Prerequisite: COSC 101, MATH 234
Course Catalog Description:
This course is about fundamental ideas in the theory of computation, including formal
languages, computability and complexity. In this course, students gain proficiency in the
concepts of automata, formal languages, grammar, algorithms, computability, decidability, and
complexity.
Textbook:
• Introduction to the Theory of Computation By Michael Sipser. Publisher: Cengage
Learning, 3rd Edition, 2012, ISBN: 978-1133187790.
Reference Materials:
1. Introduction to Automata Theory, Languages, and Computation By John E. Hopcroft,
Rajeev Motwani and Jeffrey D. Ullman. Publisher: Pearson,, 3rd Edition, 2006, ISBN:
978-0321455369.
2. Languages and Machines: An Introduction to the Theory of Computer Science By Thomas
A. Sudkamp. Publisher: Addison-Wesley Educational Publishers Inc., 2nd Edition, 2005,
ISBN: 978-0321322210.
Course Topics:
1. Finite automata: examples; definitions; designing finite automata (2 lectures)
2. Nondeterministic finite automata (2 lectures)
3. Regular expression; Pumping lemma (2 lectures)
4. Context free grammars; Pushdown automata (2 lectures)
5. Turing machines (Sec 3.1) (2 lectures)
6. Variants of Turing machines and algorithms (2 lectures)
7. Decidable languages (2 lectures)
8. Revision (1 lecture) and Midterm exam
9. Undecidability – the halting problem (2 lectures)
10. Reducibility: Undecidable Problems from Language Theory, Reductions via computation
histories (2 lectures)
11. Mapping reducibility; computable functions (2 lectures)
12. Measuring complexity; the class P (2 lectures)
13. The class NP; NP-completeness (2 lectures)
14. Additional NP-complete problems (2 lectures)
15. Revision (2 lectures)
Structure and Learning Methodologies:
The course is delivered in the form of classroom lectures and problem-solving sessions.
Lectures are 2 × 50-min per week. Tutorial sessions are 2 × 50-min per week. Individual
assignments and quizzes are held throughout the semester to reinforce knowledge of the
material.
