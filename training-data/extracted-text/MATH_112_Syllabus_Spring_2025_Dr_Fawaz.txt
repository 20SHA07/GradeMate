Course Catalog Description:
This is a second semester calculus course for students who have previously been introduced to
the basic ideas of differential and integral calculus. The emphasis in this course is on problem
solving, rather than theory. Topics include integration techniques, parametric equations,
infinite series, an introduction to vectors and vector-valued functions, as well as an introduction
to functions of several variables and double integrals.

Course Topics:
1. Integration techniques (6 lectures)
2. Improper integrals (3 lectures)
3. Parametric equations (4 lectures)
4. Polar coordinates (5 lectures)
5. Sequences of Real Numbers (3 lectures)
6. Introduction to Infinite Series (6 lectures)
7. Infinite series: testing (6 hours)
8. Power series and Taylor series (6 lectures)
9. Vectors and operations with vectors (6 lectures)
10. Planes and surfaces (6 lectures)
11. Vector-valued functions (5 lectures)
12. Introduction to functions of several variables and multiple integrals (5 lectures)
Structure and learning methodologies:
Lectures are 4 x 50 minutes per week. Lectures are presented using the whiteboard, and
may be complemented with handouts and/or PowerPoint slides.

Additional Information:
Assessments
Quizzes (3) will count for 21% of the final grade (NO DROPPED QUIZZES). Weekly
homework online assignments will count for 9%. Projects will count for 10%. Semester
Examination for 25% and Final Exam for 35%.
Project:
The project, assigned in Week 14 of the semester. Students will work in groups to investigate
a specific problem or situation assigned to them, utilizing computational tools such as
Python, MATLAB, etc. Each submission must be accompanied by a comprehensive report
that includes a description of the problem, the code implemented, the rationale behind the
chosen solution approach, the computational techniques used, and an in-depth discussion of
the outcomes. Please note that submissions made past the deadline will incur a penalty,
reducing the grade for this assessment by 10% per day late.
Calculator Policy
Only basic calculators with no graphic display will be allowed during quizzes and exams.
Classroom Policies
Classes start on time. Students arriving late to class are a distraction to both the instructor
and other students and will therefore be marked absent. It is the student’s responsibility to
sign the attendance sheet that will be handed out at the beginning of each class period. A
student that does not sign the attendance sheet will be marked “absent” in Banner, even if
present in the classroom.
Please do not sign for absent friends. Signing for another student is a serious offence and
will be treated as such.
Students are expected to bring for each class what they need for an effective lecture:
handouts (posted on Moodle), pen/pencil, notepaper, calculator etc.
Expectations from students
To succeed well in this course, students are required to work at least one hour outside the
classroom for each hour spend in class. That means four hours a week, every week. This is
an average requirement for an average student with a sufficient background. It therefore
could be more if some topics from previous courses are not known well enough.
Selected problems for each section are recommended as homework. Homework will not be
collected, but are essential part of the learning process, and are therefore mandatory.
Grading
Writing correct solutions is essential in the life of a scientist, including the correct use of the
mathematical symbols. Not only one needs to be understood by others, but only by paying
attention to what is written a student can acquire a good understanding of the concepts.
Therefore, in all assessments, the correctness of the solution writing will be evaluated,
and marks will be deducted for mistakes (notations, clarity, structure of the solution,
etc).

For any issue, the following procedure needs to be followed:
1) students need to talk first to their instructor.
2) if (1) did not resolve the issue, students can talk to the course coordinator.
3) if (1) and (2) did not resolve the issue, students can talk to the chair of the math
department.
4) if none of the above resolved the issue, students can talk to the dean of undergraduate
studies.
