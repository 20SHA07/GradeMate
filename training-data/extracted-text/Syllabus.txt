ENGL 102 Academic English II
(3 Lectures, 0 Laboratory – 3 Credits)
Prerequisite: ENGL 101
Co-requisite: None
Course Catalog Description:
This course extends skills in evidence-based reading, writing, and digital composition introduced in ENGL
101. Major assignments include writing a data-based technical report that includes a digital presentation
and a group-based funding proposal based on a realistic Request for Funding Proposal (RFP) with an oral
presentation.
Textbook:
Writing Science: How to Write Papers That Get Cited and Proposals That Get Funded by Joshua Schimel,
st
Publisher: Oxford University Press, 1 edition, 2011, ISBN: 9780199 760244
Reference Materials:
1. IEEE Editorial Style Manual (available online: https://www.ieee.org/content/dam/ieee-
org/ieee/web/org/conferences/style_references_manual.pdf)
2. University of Pittsburgh Library System: Citation Styles (available online:
https://pitt.libguides.com/citationhelp)
Course Topics:
1. Review: Writing about numbers, abstracting/summarization, citing, referencing (2 lectures)
2. Writing to specifications (6 lectures)
3. Planning/Timeline Development (3 lectures)
4. Using Excel/Writing a budget narrative (6 lectures)
5. Close reading and interpretation of research reports (6 lectures)
6. Using databases and multiple online references to support arguments (4 lectures)
7. Composing in multiple genres for a variety of audiences (8 lectures)
8. Avoiding plagiarism through paraphrasing and citation (2 lectures)
9. Advanced digital composition, including video and audio formats (8 lectures)
Structure and Learning Methodologies:
Lectures will be 2 x 75-minute sessions per week. Instruction will be a combination of lectures/seminar
discussions, writing/composing workshops, and small-group activities. Skills introduced in ENGL 101
will be extended and developed.
Laboratory and/or Computer Resources: None. Students will use their own laptops.
Assessment:
All course learning outcomes are assessed using the following assessment tools.
Individual Writing: Technical report 40%
Digital presentation of technical report 20%
Group (</= 3 students) proposal in response to a Request for Funding Proposal (RFP) 30%
Oral presentation of group proposal 10%

Additional Information:
At the discretion of the instructor, students receiving a grade of less than 70% for an assessment may
have the opportunity to revise or retake the assessment one time within a specific timeline. Note: If a
student resubmits an assignment, the maximum grade the resubmitted assignment can be awarded
is 70%.
Students are expected to attend the full class on time and participate in activities in order to be considered
present.
Students absent from in-class/live online graded assignments, writings, or quizzes will receive a zero (0)
for the activity.
Students who are found to cheat or plagiarize (copy in any form) text, images, or other materials may
receive a zero or a grade reduction and a report to the Chair of English and the Dean of the College of
Arts and Sciences. Depending on the classification of the violation, the case may be forwarded to the
Academic Integrity Council for review. Therefore, students are advised to consult the Handbook for all
policies related to plagiarism and other forms of cheating, especially ACA 3500.
