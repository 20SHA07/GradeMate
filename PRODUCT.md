# Product

## Register

product

## Users

GradeMate is for Khalifa University students who need a fast, trustworthy way to understand GPA, course grades, syllabus assessment weights, and target scores. Students may use it casually as guests for quick GPA calculations, or as signed-in users for a fuller semester workspace.

Users are often checking grades between classes, before exams, or while reading a syllabus. They need the interface to stay calm, compact, and obvious, especially on phones and laptops.

## Product Purpose

GradeMate helps students track courses, calculate GPA, import KU Course Library templates, extract syllabus grading breakdowns, and plan the scores they need for target grades.

Success means a student can open the app, add or import a course, understand their current standing, scan or paste a syllabus, review detected assessments, and save only confirmed structured data without feeling overwhelmed.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, student-friendly.

GradeMate should feel like a premium student workspace: focused, trustworthy, and useful without trying too hard. It should have the confidence of a serious academic tool, but stay approachable for friends beta-testing it.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboard templates.
- AI-looking feature panels, vague automation copy, or "AI slop".
- Huge cards, oversized heroes inside the app, and marketing layouts where students need tools.
- Random gradients, decorative blobs, glass effects, and visual noise.
- Cluttered university portals.
- Truncated sidebar labels or chopped table text.
- Raw technical copy, API errors, auth internals, Edge Function errors, or broken service messages.

## Design Principles

1. Calm by default, powerful on demand.
   Keep the main view quiet. Put advanced tools behind tabs, modals, accordions, or secondary actions.

2. Trust through review.
   Syllabus extraction should always feel like a confirmation experience, not an automatic black box. Show what was detected, what needs review, and what will be saved.

3. Student workflow first.
   Optimize for common student tasks: add a course, check GPA, import a template, scan a syllabus, see target grade requirements, and come back later.

4. Compact but readable.
   Dense information is allowed when it helps comparison and scanning. Tables, cards, and sidebars should be tight without becoming cramped.

5. Privacy is part of the product.
   PDF handling, guest mode, and save behavior should make students feel safe. Normal PDFs are read locally and not stored.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

GradeMate should be mobile-first, keyboard usable, and readable in stressful study contexts. Focus states must be visible, buttons need clear labels, status should not rely on color alone, and tables should scroll inside their containers on small screens. The dark theme should maintain strong contrast with off-white text and muted secondary copy.
